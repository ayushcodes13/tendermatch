# Manufacturer matching stages

The matcher now exposes candidate generation and ranking as separate operations in `matching/matcher.py`:

1. `generate_candidates(tender)` embeds the tender and computes manufacturer-specific lexical evidence, including keywords, products, categories, aliases, concepts, service terms, and negative terms.
2. The current catalog has 14 profiles, so candidate generation retains the full catalog for ranking. It does not apply a semantic threshold or discard a candidate because of one negative phrase. The candidate pool contains the raw semantic score and structured evidence for each profile.
3. `rank_candidates(candidate_pool, top_k=...)` applies the existing evidence boosts and negative penalties, then sorts the pool. `match_topk` remains the compatibility entry point and composes both stages.

Keeping the full small catalog preserves recall at candidate generation and makes its output inspectable. It does not establish that the final top-k ranking has good precision or technical compatibility. Scores remain heuristic, and tenders may require human review. Eligibility, conflicting specifications, context-aware domain exclusions, and calibrated thresholds remain later roadmap work.

`classify_tender` returns a structured `explanation` and includes the same object in its decision trace. It separates embedding similarity, product/category/alias agreement, technical concepts, service and negative evidence, decision reasons, and the overall heuristic score. It explicitly marks source quality as `not_assessed` and lists eligibility, specification compliance, availability, and delivery as unverified. Manufacturer source links are passed through when the profile records them; the catalog now records profile-level source links, which do not yet map each capability claim to its own source. No score is represented as a probability.

Decision bucket cutoffs are stored in `data/matching_thresholds.json`. The standard-library loader validates known keys, integer ranges, and threshold ordering. Manufacturer semantic-evidence boundaries are stored as percentage points on the current 0–1 heuristic score (55 and 78 by default). `classify_tender(..., thresholds={"explore": 60})` can apply a validated per-call override, and each trace records the effective thresholds for reproducibility. The default file preserves the existing decision cutoffs. Changing thresholds does not imply calibrated confidence or improved matching quality; use the golden-data sensitivity evaluation and report its incomplete synthetic label scope.

`data/evaluation/baseline_synthetic_2026-10-22.json` is the historical pre-profile-correction diagnostic. `data/evaluation/baseline_synthetic_nippon_fertilizer_context_2026-10-22.json` is the latest synthetic matcher output. Adding a source-backed fertilizer mercury-analysis application causes Nippon to rank first for the synthetic analyzer tender, while a contextual fertilizer-commodity rule moves it to third for the synthetic bulk-fertilizer-only tender, retaining the candidate but lowering its rank. Across the 18-case synthetic set, all 8 labeled relevant pairs remain in the top five; 8/11 retrieved definitively labeled pairs are relevant, with 68 candidates unjudged. This is diagnostic evidence only; it is not a production quality claim, and the dataset has zero human-reviewed labels. The bulk-fertilizer case remains a labeled false positive in the top-five retrieval set and is recorded for human review; this rule does not hard-exclude candidates. The deterministic matcher tests verify that lexical candidates remain in the generated pool even when their semantic score is low, inactive profiles are skipped, and the ranking stage limits only the returned top-k list.
